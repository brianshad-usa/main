When Your Business Needs a vCIO

Long before a company is big enough to hire a Chief Information Officer, it starts making CIO-sized decisions. These are the growth stages and warning signs that mean it is time for strategic IT leadership.

The need for a vCIO rarely announces itself. It shows up as a run of decisions no one quite owns — a budget defended by habit, a security question deferred, a purchase made in a hurry. Any one of them is survivable. The pattern is the signal.

The warning signs

If several of these are familiar, the business has likely outgrown running technology without a strategy:

  • You are growing, hiring or opening a location — and technology keeps arriving late to those changes.
  • Your technology budget is really last year’s copied forward, with no roadmap behind it.
  • Nobody owns security strategy, and cyber-insurance or client questionnaires are getting harder to answer honestly.
  • You keep getting surprised by costs when equipment fails without a replacement plan.
  • Vendors and renewals pile up with no one actively managing the relationships or the spend.
  • Leadership is making technology calls it does not feel equipped to make, because there is no one else to make them.

The growth stages where it usually starts

There is no magic headcount, but the need tends to appear at recognizable moments:

  1. Past the founder-does-IT stage. Technology is now too important and too complex for it to be someone’s side responsibility.
  2. Adding people or places. Every new hire and location multiplies decisions about access, capacity, security and cost.
  3. A compliance or insurance trigger. A client contract, an audit, or a cyber-insurance renewal starts asking questions that need an owner.
  4. After a scare. An outage, a close call, or a bill that should have been planned for makes the cost of no strategy concrete.

The best time to bring in a vCIO is before the trigger, not after — a plan built calmly is cheaper and better than one built in a crisis. Our free technology-plan framework is a good place to start the thinking today.

What changes once you have one

With a vCIO in place, the budget starts tracing to the business instead of to last year, replacements get scheduled instead of sprung, security becomes a plan rather than a worry, and someone is accountable for the direction. The help desk and managed IT still handle the day to day — but now it all points somewhere. For how the models fit together, see vCIO vs. break-fix vs. managed IT.
