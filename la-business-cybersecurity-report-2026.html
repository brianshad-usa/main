Los Angeles Business Cybersecurity Report 2026

What the major 2026 research says about cyber threats, what it means for Los Angeles industries, and the controls worth funding first.

Executive summary

The state of cybersecurity for Los Angeles businesses in 2026

This report summarizes the year’s major research for organizations with 5 to 500 employees in the Greater Los Angeles area: current threats, industry risks, the regulations that apply by industry, and practical protection strategies.

Three findings stand out. Unpatched vulnerabilities are now the most common way attackers get in, ahead of phishing and stolen passwords. Ransomware appears in nearly half of confirmed breaches, and small organizations are disproportionately affected. Business email compromise remains the second-costliest crime reported to the FBI, and California reports more complaints and larger losses than any other state.

Section 1

Current cyber threats targeting LA businesses

Exploited vulnerabilities

Exploitation of known vulnerabilities is now the most common initial access vector in Verizon’s 2026 Data Breach Investigations Report: 31% of breaches, against 16% for phishing and 13% for credential abuse. Among small and mid-sized businesses, vulnerabilities also lead, at 26%. Only 26% of the vulnerabilities on CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list were fully remediated in 2025. Firewalls, VPN gateways and other systems that face the internet deserve the first deadline.

Ransomware

Ransomware was present in 48% of breaches in the same report, and 69% of the victims in its data did not pay. Attackers encrypt patient records, legal files, production assets and financial systems, and often threaten to publish what they copied first, so a clean restore does not always end the incident. Backups still decide how long the business is down.

Business email compromise (BEC)

Attackers impersonate executives, vendors or attorneys, often from a real mailbox they have compromised, to redirect wire transfers, request payments or change payroll details. The FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center recorded more than $3 billion in business email compromise losses in 2025, second only to investment fraud. Firms that move large sums on emailed instructions, such as real estate, legal, entertainment and financial practices, have the most to review.

Phishing and credential theft

Phishing opened 16% of breaches and stolen or abused credentials another 13%, and a human element was present in 62%. Multi-factor authentication, email security and training remain essential. The change in 2026 is that they no longer cover the most common way in.

Supply chain attacks

Third parties were involved in 48% of breaches, and in 55% of those at small and mid-sized businesses. A Los Angeles company may have strong internal controls and still be exposed through a managed service provider, payroll vendor or cloud application. Reviewing the security of each third party that can reach your systems or data is now a basic part of a security program.

Insider threats

Not all incidents begin outside the organization. A departing employee may copy data, and a well-meaning one may misconfigure a storage folder or send a sensitive file to a personal account. Access controls, least privilege, training and activity monitoring reduce the risk, implemented in a way that respects employee privacy under California law.

Section 2

Industry-specific cyber risk in Los Angeles

Healthcare
Greater Los Angeles has a large concentration of independent medical practices, dental offices, behavioral health providers and specialty clinics. They hold records that cannot be reissued and they cannot simply stop operating, so an attack can delay care as well as expose data. HIPAA requires covered entities and business associates to implement administrative, physical and technical safeguards. See our healthcare IT services.
Legal
Law firms hold litigation strategy, transaction documents, financial disclosures and privileged communications. A breach can expose a firm to malpractice claims and professional discipline, and fraud aimed at wire instructions is a persistent threat. In May 2026 the FBI described a group that targets US law firms by posing as their own IT support.
Entertainment and media
Unreleased films, scripts, music masters and visual effects projects represent significant value, and production and post-production companies work to fixed release dates. Tight deadlines and large shared file systems make downtime expensive and recovery plans important.
Manufacturing and aerospace
In Verizon’s 2026 manufacturing data, exploited vulnerabilities opened 38% of breaches and third parties were involved in 61%. Suppliers that handle controlled unclassified information (CUI) must meet NIST SP 800-171 where their contracts require it. The Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) program adds verification; the Pentagon suspended its second phase for review in July 2026, so confirm the current position with your prime. Our aerospace and defense IT services cover the IT side: access controls, MFA, patching, monitoring and backups verified by restore testing.
Professional services
Accounting firms, financial advisors, real estate brokerages and consulting firms handle financial data and personal information under a growing set of rules. Enterprise clients increasingly ask vendors for a SOC 2 report or equivalent evidence before granting access to their systems.

Section 4

Compliance requirements by industry

For the risk assessments, policies and audits these frameworks require, we work alongside your compliance advisor and handle the IT side.

HIPAA
Applies to healthcare providers, health plans and their business associates. Requires administrative, physical and technical safeguards for protected health information. Civil penalties can reach into the millions of dollars.
PCI DSS
Applies to businesses that accept, process, store or transmit payment card data, including retailers, restaurants, medical offices and service providers. Requirements are enforced through the card brands and acquiring banks.
CMMC
Applies through contract clauses to suppliers that handle federal contract information or controlled unclassified information. Phase II of the program, which adds third-party assessments, was suspended for review in July 2026. NIST SP 800-171 obligations in existing contracts remain.
SOC 2
An attestation report that enterprise clients often request from SaaS companies, managed service providers and professional services firms. A Type II report describes whether controls operated effectively over a period.
CCPA
The California Consumer Privacy Act applies to businesses that collect personal information about California residents and meet its thresholds. It grants consumers rights over their data and requires reasonable security. California also sets deadlines for breach notices; counsel can confirm what applies.

Section 5

Cybersecurity checklist for LA businesses

Nine controls, in the order the 2026 findings suggest, consistent with guidance from CISA and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework.

  1. Patch internet-facing systems first. Keep an inventory of firewalls, VPN gateways, remote access tools and servers, and give every entry on CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list a deadline and an owner.
  2. Multi-factor authentication on all accounts. Enforce it on email, cloud applications, remote access and administrative accounts, with phishing-resistant methods for administrators.
  3. Endpoint detection and response (EDR). Behavioral monitoring and automated response on desktops, laptops and servers, with someone responsible for alerts after hours.
  4. Email security and anti-phishing. Filtering, anti-spoofing controls (SPF, DKIM and DMARC) and link protection.
  5. Payment verification. Confirm new or changed payment instructions by calling a number on file, every time, regardless of who asks.
  6. Security awareness training. Ongoing practice in reporting and verifying, including voice and text messages.
  7. Encrypted, isolated and tested backup. Keep a copy the production network cannot alter, and prove recovery with restore tests.
  8. Incident response plan. A written, rehearsed plan for the first hours: who to call, how to isolate systems, what evidence to preserve, and when to engage counsel and your insurer. Ours is published in full.
  9. Vendor review and cyber insurance. Review each third party with access to your systems or data, and check that coverage limits and exclusions match what an incident would cost.

Common questions

Cybersecurity for LA businesses, answered

What are the biggest cybersecurity threats to LA businesses in 2026?

Exploited vulnerabilities, ransomware, business email compromise, phishing and compromised vendors. In Verizon's 2026 Data Breach Investigations Report, exploitation of vulnerabilities was the most common way in (31% of breaches), followed by phishing (16%) and credential abuse (13%), and ransomware was present in 48% of breaches. The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center recorded $3.05 billion in business email compromise losses in 2025, second only to investment fraud.

How much does a data breach cost a small business?

There is no single reliable figure. IBM's 2026 Cost of a Data Breach Report puts the global average at $4.99 million across 602 organizations worldwide, which is a direction rather than a budget number for a small business. The cost depends on the data involved and how quickly the breach is found and contained, and typically includes incident response, downtime, lost productivity, legal fees, regulatory penalties and customer notification.

What cybersecurity compliance rules apply to Los Angeles businesses?

It depends on your industry and the data you handle. Healthcare providers and their business associates must comply with HIPAA. Businesses that process card payments must meet PCI DSS requirements. Defense suppliers that handle controlled unclassified information must meet NIST SP 800-171 where their contracts require it; the CMMC program adds verification, and its second phase was suspended for review in July 2026. SaaS companies and professional firms serving enterprise clients are often asked for a SOC 2 report. Businesses that meet the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) thresholds are subject to its rules on personal information. A qualified compliance advisor can confirm which frameworks apply to your business.

How can a small business protect itself from ransomware?

Protection is layered, not a single product. The most important steps are patching internet-facing systems promptly, because exploited vulnerabilities are now the most common way in; multi-factor authentication on all accounts, especially email and remote access; endpoint detection and response on all devices; encrypted, isolated backups proven by restore tests; training that gives employees a simple way to report and verify; and a rehearsed incident response plan that covers data theft as well as encryption.

Does my small business really need managed cybersecurity?

It depends on whether you can keep the controls running. Someone has to patch on schedule, watch alerts outside business hours, test restores and rehearse the response. Organizations that can staff that work may not need a provider; those that cannot usually share it with one. What matters is that every control has a named owner and a date when it was last tested.
