What Does a vCIO Do?

A virtual CIO owns the direction of a company’s technology — the planning, budgeting and decisions that a Chief Information Officer would make, on a schedule a growing business can afford.

A virtual CIO (vCIO) does the strategic half of technology — the part that a help desk and a managed IT contract do not cover. Where support keeps things working, a vCIO decides what to build, what to replace, what to protect and what to spend, and makes sure those decisions add up to a direction. Here is what that work looks like in practice.

The core responsibilities

  • Technology planning & roadmap. A rolling one-to-three-year plan that starts with where the business is going and works back to what technology needs to be true to get there. It is the same discipline behind our free technology-plan framework, run all year rather than at budget season.
  • Budgeting. A technology budget where every line traces to something the business is doing, replacements are scheduled instead of reactive, and resilience is a chosen line item.
  • Security strategy. Deciding where the risk actually sits and what to address first — backup and tested recovery, endpoint protection, identity and access, and a written response plan — then handing the work to the security team to carry out.
  • Vendor management. One point of accountability for the vendors, licenses and renewals a business accumulates, so contracts are managed rather than just paid.
  • Compliance guidance. Practical help understanding the obligations that apply to your industry and preparing the environment to meet them, without overbuilding for rules that do not apply.

What a vCIO is not

A vCIO is not your help desk, and not a salesperson for hardware. The value is judgment: someone who has seen several years of technology decisions play out and can tell the difference between a purchase the business needs and one it merely got sold. The day-to-day support still runs through managed IT and the help desk; the vCIO makes sure it points somewhere.
