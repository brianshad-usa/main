What a Microsoft 365 Security Assessment Checks (and How to Order One Online)

A Microsoft 365 security assessment reviews about 80 settings across identity, email, sharing and devices. Here's what gets checked and how ours works.

Brian ShadFounder & CEO, Pro Link Systems

4 min read

In this article

  2. The 13 areas we check

  4. What you receive

  6. Ordering

Most Microsoft 365 tenants were not designed so much as accumulated. A setting chosen during a migration, a guest invited for one project, an admin role granted "temporarily", an app someone approved in a hurry. None of it looks dangerous on its own, and none of it shows up on an invoice. Together it decides how far an attacker gets with one phished password.

A security assessment answers a narrower question than most IT projects: where does your tenant actually stand today? Done properly, it changes nothing while it looks.

What a security assessment is, and what it isn't

Ours is a one-time, read-only review of about 80 security controls in your Microsoft 365. It is not a penetration test: nobody tries to break in. It is not a migration or a cleanup either. We read your configuration, compare each control with how it should be set, and report what we find, sorted by severity.

Read-only matters. Your Global Administrator grants access through Microsoft's own consent screen and assigns Microsoft's view-only Global Reader role. We never ask for an account or a password, we don't change any setting, and we don't open the content of email or files.

The 13 areas we check

  • Identity and multi-factor authentication. Who can sign in without MFA, and whether the methods in use hold up against phishing.
  • Conditional Access. Whether policies actually block risky sign-ins or only record them, and who is excluded.
  • Privileged and admin access. How many people hold standing admin rights, and whether they need them.
  • User account hygiene. Former employees, unused accounts and shared mailboxes that can still sign in.
  • External and guest access. Who outside the company can reach your data, and whether anyone reviews that list.
  • App consents. Third-party apps that users or admins have allowed into mail, files or the directory.
  • Email security. Exchange Online protection, anti-phishing settings, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC for your domain.
  • SharePoint and OneDrive sharing. Anonymous links, organization-wide sharing defaults and link expiry.
  • Microsoft Teams. External chat, guest access and who can create new teams.
  • Microsoft Defender. Which protections you are licensed for, and which are actually turned on.
  • Device management (Intune). Whether company data is reached only from devices you manage.
  • Data protection (Microsoft Purview). Sensitivity labels and data loss prevention, where your licenses include them.
  • Audit logging. Whether the logs you would need after an incident are turned on and kept.

How it works

  1. Order and pay online. A short form and a secure card payment through Authorize.net.
  2. Approve read-only access. Your Global Administrator completes two steps from our email, in about five minutes.
  3. We approve and run the assessment. Our team approves every engagement before it runs. If we decline, you get a full refund.
  4. We review every finding. A person on our team checks each finding before your report is released.
  5. Your report and review call. You receive the report and book a complimentary call to go through it with us.

What you receive

A findings report sorted by severity, so the items that matter most come first. You can see the format in our sample report (PDF, sample data). The report tells you what we found and how serious each finding is. What you do about it is your decision: your own team, your current provider, or us.

When it makes sense

  • Before rolling out Microsoft Copilot, which can surface anything a user's account is able to reach.
  • When you have inherited a tenant from a previous provider or administrator.
  • After changes in your IT staff, or when two companies' tenants are being combined.
  • When you want a baseline to measure later work against.
