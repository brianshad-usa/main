Microsoft 365 vs Google Workspace: Which Is Better for Los Angeles Businesses?

The choice between Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace turns on how your people work, your existing systems and the security controls your plan includes.

Pro Link SystemsInsights
June 3, 2026 · Updated November 21, 2026
5 min read
In this article

  2. Check your existing systems
  3. Security: settings matter more than brand
  4. Regulated data and legal agreements
  5. Cost beyond the subscription
  6. A simple way to decide

Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace both cover email, calendars, file storage, documents and video meetings, so the choice rarely turns on a feature list. It turns on your existing systems, how your people work, what you must protect, and who will administer it. This comparison is for owners and IT leaders at Los Angeles businesses choosing between Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace for the first time, or considering a switch. It deliberately leaves out price tables, because plans, bundles and discounts change often; confirm current pricing with each vendor or a reseller.

Start with how your people actually work

The most useful comparison is a day in the life of your busiest teams. Finance staff who build large spreadsheets with macros and external data links, or a law office that exchanges tracked-change documents with clients, will likely feel the difference between installed Office applications and browser-based editors. Teams that mostly co-author short documents, share files and meet by video may prefer a browser-first tool. This is analysis rather than a measured result: the practical test is to give a few real users, including your most demanding one, a trial of each for two weeks and record what they could not do.

When you compare plans, check whether each tier includes installed desktop applications or web versions only, how mailbox and file storage are allocated, what meeting limits apply, and what is included for security. Those details vary by plan and change over time.

Check your existing systems

Existing infrastructure often decides the question. A company with Windows PCs, Active Directory on its own servers and applications that integrate with Microsoft tools will usually find Microsoft 365 an easier fit. A company with a mix of Macs, Chromebooks and phones, no on-site servers, and mostly web-based business applications may find Google Workspace simpler to run. Neither is impossible in the other's environment; the cost is in integration and training. List the third-party applications you depend on, such as accounting, case management or a practice-management system, and ask each vendor which suite it integrates with and how.

Security: settings matter more than brand

Both platforms offer multi-factor authentication, encryption and administrative controls. Whether they protect you depends on configuration, which is the customer's responsibility. Microsoft states that customers always retain responsibility for their data, accounts and access management, and the same principle applies to any software-as-a-service product. Two points are worth checking in a proposal:

  • Licensing tier determines which controls you get. Microsoft's Conditional Access, which makes access decisions from signals such as device and location, requires Microsoft Entra ID P1 licensing, and Microsoft 365 Business Premium customers can also use it. Security defaults are available to all customers. Ask which tier covers the controls you need.
  • Sign-in strength. The US cybersecurity agency CISA strongly urges phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication. Confirm that your platform and plan support it for all staff, and at a minimum for administrators.

If you already use Microsoft 365, a review of your current settings can show whether you are using the protection you pay for.

Regulated data and legal agreements

Healthcare, legal and financial firms should treat the contract as part of the choice. For health information, Microsoft offers a business associate agreement through its data protection terms for in-scope services, and Google requires customers to accept one before using protected health information with covered Workspace services. Both say the customer remains responsible for how it uses the services; Microsoft states plainly that using its services does not on its own make a customer compliant with HIPAA. Which agreements and settings your practice needs is a question for your attorney or compliance adviser. For the technical side, see healthcare IT services.

Cost beyond the subscription

The monthly license is only part of the cost. Include migration of mail and files, training, add-on security and backup tools, administrative time, and the cost of leaving if you change your mind. Ask your provider to show the three-year cost under each option, with assumptions stated. If you have offices in places such as Santa Monica or Century City that share files, include the time to move shared drives and reconnect them.

A simple way to decide

Questions that point toward one platform or the other (analysis, not a ranking)
QuestionIf yes, lean towardVerify
Do key staff depend on advanced desktop spreadsheet or document features?Microsoft 365Test their real files on the web and desktop versions
Do you run Windows PCs and on-premises Active Directory?Microsoft 365Plan for identity synchronization and who administers it
Is your fleet mixed, with many Macs or Chromebooks and web-based tools?Google WorkspaceConfirm that your line-of-business applications work well in a browser
Do you handle regulated data?Either, subject to the agreementGet the business associate or equivalent agreement reviewed by counsel
Do you need specific security controls such as Conditional Access?Depends on the planList the controls and get the plan that includes them in writing

Whichever platform you pick, decide who administers it. A managed IT services provider or internal IT support team should own the setup, security settings and review schedule, and a working-from-day-one checklist should include MFA on every account and a tested backup of mail and files.
