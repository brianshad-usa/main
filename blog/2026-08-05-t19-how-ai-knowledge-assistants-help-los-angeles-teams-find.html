AI Knowledge Assistants for LA Teams

An assistant works when good documents exist and people cannot find them. Here is what it cannot do, how to scope it and how to measure a pilot.

What an AI knowledge assistant does, and when it helps

An AI knowledge assistant answers employee questions in plain language using documents your company already has: policies, procedures, IT instructions and product material. This article is for operations and IT leaders at Los Angeles teams who field repeated questions through email, chat and the help desk, and who are deciding whether an assistant is worth piloting. It helps most when the answers already exist in good documents that people cannot find. It helps little when the documents are outdated or missing, because the assistant will surface the gaps faster than it fills them.

What it can and cannot do

These assistants search your content, pull relevant passages and write an answer, often with links back to the source. Typical uses are policy questions ("How many days notice for time off?"), routine IT steps ("How do I connect to the office printer?") and finding who owns a process. They can also route a question to a person when they lack an answer, if you set that up. Limits matter as much as strengths:

  • They can be wrong. Generative AI can produce confident but incorrect statements. Microsoft's own documentation says Copilot responses are not guaranteed to be fully factual and that users should review output before relying on it. The US National Institute of Standards and Technology maintains a Generative AI Profile to help organizations identify risks specific to this technology.
  • They reflect your documents. An old policy produces an old answer. Someone must own content freshness.
  • They inherit your permissions. Microsoft states that Copilot only surfaces data a user already has permission to view. If a folder is shared too widely, the assistant will quote it to anyone who asks.
  • They do not replace people for judgement calls. Anything involving employment decisions, legal interpretation or exceptions should go to a person.

Decide what content goes in

Start with a narrow, approved set: the employee handbook, IT how-to articles, standard operating procedures and onboarding material. Leave out anything sensitive until permissions are reviewed. Assign an owner to each source, with a review date, and decide how a wrong answer gets reported and fixed. For companies on Microsoft 365, where documents live in SharePoint and Teams, the assistant can work with that content directly; for others the integration is a project of its own, so ask what connections the product supports and who maintains them. See your cloud services setup for where documents actually live.

Set rules for sensitive and regulated information

Ask where the data is processed, how long prompts and answers are retained, whether they are used to train models, and whether administrators can see usage logs. For organizations with regulated data, such as health records, involve your attorney or compliance adviser before connecting any assistant to it. The specific agreements and safeguards depend on your obligations, and a vendor's general statements are not a substitute for that review. Your cybersecurity provider can check access settings, and teams in healthcare can start from healthcare IT services for the technical environment.

Run a pilot and measure it

Do not accept a vendor's claimed ticket reduction as your own. Measure your baseline for a month, then pilot with one department and compare. Useful measures:

What to measure in a pilot
MeasureHow to collect itWhat it tells you
Repeat questions to IT or HRCount tickets and messages on the top ten topics before and during the pilotWhether the assistant is actually deflecting routine questions
Answer accuracyHave a subject expert grade a sample of answers each weekWhether it is safe to widen use
Escalation qualityReview whether handed-off questions include enough contextWhether staff avoid repeating themselves
Content gaps foundList questions the assistant could not answerWhich documents to write or update
User confidenceShort survey after the first monthWhether staff trust and use it

A pilot of six to eight weeks is usually enough to see whether routine questions fall and whether accuracy is acceptable. Define in advance what result would make you expand, keep as is or stop. Be wary of any figure for expected savings that is not based on your own tickets and your own documents.

Who should own it

Give the assistant a business owner, not only an IT owner. IT can manage access, integration and logging, but the people who write the policies must keep the answers current. If you use managed IT services, agree who handles which tasks: access reviews, content updates, answering the questions the assistant cannot, and the quarterly review of the measures above.
