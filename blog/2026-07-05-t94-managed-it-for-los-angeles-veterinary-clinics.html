Managed IT for LA Veterinary Clinics

HIPAA is not the usual veterinary rule; here are the record-retention rules that apply, what to protect first and what to ask an IT provider.

Pro Link SystemsInsights
July 5, 2026 · Updated November 21, 2026
4 min read
In this article
  1. Which rules actually apply to a veterinary clinic
  2. What a clinic should protect first
  3. Backups, ransomware and keeping the schedule running
  4. Integration with practice software and equipment
  5. Choosing IT support for a Los Angeles veterinary clinic

Managed IT for Los Angeles veterinary clinics is mostly a records and uptime problem: the practice management system, the digital radiographs and the lab results are the medical record, and a clinic with an open schedule cannot work without them. This article is for clinic owners and practice managers who need to decide what the legal baseline for records is, which systems to protect first and what to ask an IT provider.

Which rules actually apply to a veterinary clinic

HIPAA is often assumed to apply to veterinary clinics, and that assumption needs care. HIPAA's coverage is defined in 45 CFR 160.103 in terms of health plans, health care clearinghouses and health care providers that transmit certain standard transactions electronically, and the rule concerns health information about individuals. Animal medical records are not what it was written for, though a clinic should ask its attorney whether any part of its operations is covered.

The rules a California veterinary clinic can rely on more directly concern the records themselves. The Veterinary Medical Board's frequently asked questions state that patient medical records must be kept for three years after the date of the last visit, that radiographs are part of the patient record and follow the same period, and that under Business and Professions Code section 4855 a veterinarian must give the client a summary of the record on request, either as a copy or a summary with key medical and drug information. Clinics also hold owners' names, addresses, phone numbers and payment cards, which carry duties under California's breach notification law and card-brand rules. Counsel can say which apply to your clinic.

What a clinic should protect first

Five things matter most, roughly in this order. The ranking is analysis rather than a published standard.

  • The practice management database, including medical notes, vaccination history, prescriptions and the client ledger
  • Digital radiographs and ultrasound or dental images, which often live in a separate application and storage location from the main database
  • Laboratory and diagnostic results, which may arrive from in-house analyzers or from outside labs through an integration
  • The credentials of every person who can open them, and the vendors that have remote access to the server
  • The ability to take payments if the connection fails

For each, ask where the data physically sits (a clinic server, a vendor cloud, a workstation), who maintains it, and how it comes back after a loss. A clinic that has one server in a back room and a backup drive plugged into the same server has one point of failure.

Backups, ransomware and keeping the schedule running

Ransomware and hardware failure end the same way for a clinic: no access to records. CISA's #StopRansomware Guide recommends offline, encrypted backups and regular testing that they can actually be restored, noting that ransomware often tries to find and delete or encrypt backups it can reach. It also recommends phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication on email and remote access. Pro Link Systems verifies backups by restoring them; ask any provider for the date of the last restore and what was restored, for example a recent patient's full record with radiographs, rather than a status report.

  • Restore one recent patient record and its radiographs from backup, and write down the date and elapsed time
  • Confirm that records are retained for at least the period the Veterinary Medical Board states, and who may delete them
  • Confirm that the backup is not reachable from the same login that runs the server day to day
  • List every vendor with remote access and who at the clinic approves each connection

Plan a downtime routine too. If the system is down for half a day, how are check-ins, treatment notes and prescriptions handled on paper, and who enters them afterward? A one-page procedure, printed and kept at the front desk, matters more in that moment than any technical control.

Integration with practice software and equipment

Veterinary software and imaging equipment often come from different vendors, and the provider's job is to keep them working together without changing what the vendors support. Before any operating system upgrade, server move or new network equipment, ask the practice software and imaging vendors in writing what versions they support. Windows 10 reached end of support on October 14, 2025, so any workstation or server still running it needs an upgrade path or enrollment in Microsoft's paid Extended Security Updates program, with the vendor's confirmation that the application works on the replacement.

Choosing IT support for a Los Angeles veterinary clinic

Ask for a provider that will talk directly to your software vendor, work around your busiest days, and give a straight answer about what it does not cover. Ask who answers on a Saturday when the system fails, and whether the provider can reach the clinic in person. A multi-site group should ask how new locations are brought onto the same standards.

Judge the arrangement by a few numbers: the date and result of the last restore test, the number of unplanned outages in a quarter and how long each lasted, how many machines are on unsupported software, and whether departed staff lost access the same day.

References

  • 45 CFR 160.103, Definitions, Electronic Code of Federal Regulations.
  • Frequently Asked Questions, Veterinary Medical Board of California.
  • #StopRansomware Guide, CISA.
  • Extended Security Updates (ESU) program for Windows 10, Microsoft Learn.
