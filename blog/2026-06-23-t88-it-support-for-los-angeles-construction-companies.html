IT Support for LA Construction Companies

Construction IT risk comes from constant churn in users, devices and payments; this covers device control, payment verification and a job-site setup process.

Pro Link SystemsInsights
June 23, 2026 · Updated November 21, 2026
4 min read
In this article
  1. Why construction IT differs from office IT
  2. Field devices and access control
  3. Standing up a job site
  4. Ransomware, backups and recovery time
  5. Choosing IT support for a Los Angeles construction company

IT support for Los Angeles construction companies is difficult because the company's technology is spread across a main office, temporary trailers, personal phones and tablets, and dozens of outside parties who need project access for a few months at a time. This article is for owners, controllers and operations managers who need to decide how field devices are managed, how payments are protected and what a job-site setup should include.

Why construction IT differs from office IT

An office has one network and a stable list of users. A contractor has a project that opens, runs for a year and closes, with a superintendent on site, subcontractor staff who are not employees, an owner's representative, and sometimes a lender's inspector, all needing documents. Most of the risk comes from that churn: accounts created in a hurry and never removed, files shared by link, and personal phones holding drawings and email.

Money is the second difference. Payment applications, change orders, lien waivers and subcontractor invoices move by email in large amounts, so a criminal who controls one mailbox can redirect a payment. The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center describes business email compromise as criminals taking over real email accounts to send unauthorized transfer requests, and advises confirming any change in account information through a separate channel. A written rule that bank details are never changed on the strength of an email, followed by a call to a known number, costs nothing to adopt.

Field devices and access control

Field staff use phones and tablets, often their own. Microsoft's documentation explains that Intune app protection policies can protect company data inside apps such as email and OneDrive without enrolling the whole device, for example by requiring a PIN, blocking copy and paste into personal apps and allowing a selective wipe of company data. That suits personal devices and subcontractor-adjacent staff better than full device management. Company-owned laptops and tablets can be fully managed.

On the sign-in side, Microsoft Entra Conditional Access can require multifactor authentication, or require a compliant device, before access to Microsoft 365 is granted. Conditional Access requires Microsoft Entra ID P1 licensing, which Microsoft says Microsoft 365 Business Premium includes. Ask which licenses you own before assuming these controls are available.

Standing up a job site

  1. Decide connectivity before mobilization. Order the trailer's internet service early and plan a cellular backup; a project engineer working offline needs documents synced beforehand.
  2. Put the trailer on its own network. Keep site Wi-Fi and cameras separate from accounting and the main office, and do not share the guest password with subcontractors.
  3. Create project access from a list. Each outside user gets a named account with only the project folders they need and an end date.
  4. Protect the devices. Apply app protection or device management before staff install email and the project management app.
  5. Close out access at substantial completion. Remove outside accounts, rotate shared credentials, and archive the project files to storage that is backed up.

Ransomware, backups and recovery time

Ransomware locks the files a project depends on: drawings, schedules, estimates and payroll. CISA's #StopRansomware Guide recommends offline, encrypted backups, regular testing that they can be restored and phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication on email and remote access. For a contractor, the useful question is how long the company can run without its estimating and accounting systems. Write that number down, and ask the provider to show a restore of one project folder and the accounting file against it.

Choosing IT support for a Los Angeles construction company

Ask whether the provider has supported a contractor with field staff and rotating subcontractors, and how it handles a new project, an outside user who leaves and a lost tablet on a Saturday. Ask about on-site response for a trailer or office, and who answers when a superintendent cannot sign in at 6 a.m. Support for the project management platform you use is partly the software vendor's job; the provider should handle the sign-in, devices and integrations around it. Judge results by the time to create and remove project accounts, the number of lost devices wiped within an hour of being reported, restore test results, and the number of payment-change requests verified by call-back.
