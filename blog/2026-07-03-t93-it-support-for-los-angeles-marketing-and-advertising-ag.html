IT Support for LA Marketing Agencies

An agency holds its clients' brand accounts and files; this covers access control, security questionnaires, email authentication and what to ask a provider.

Pro Link SystemsInsights
July 3, 2026 · Updated November 21, 2026
4 min read
In this article

  2. Checklist: before a client's security review
  3. Large files, collaboration and software

  5. Choosing IT support for a Los Angeles agency

IT support for Los Angeles marketing and advertising agencies has an unusual exposure: an agency holds the keys to its clients' brands. It manages their ad accounts, social channels, websites, email platforms and unreleased campaign files, usually through a mix of staff, freelancers and contractors. This article is for agency owners and operations heads who need to decide how client access is controlled, how large creative files move, and what to say when a client sends a security questionnaire.

Why agency IT risk is about client access

In most businesses, a stolen password exposes the company's own data. In an agency, it can expose a client's advertising budget, customer lists or a product launch that is still confidential. Account takeover is therefore the central risk, and it is made worse by common agency habits: shared logins to client platforms, credentials stored in a team spreadsheet, freelancers who keep access after a project ends, and personal devices used for work. These patterns are an observation about agency operations, not a measured statistic.

The control that addresses most of them is multi-factor authentication. CISA's #StopRansomware Guide recommends phishing-resistant MFA for services such as email and VPNs and says to escalate any system that cannot enforce it to senior management. For a platform that does not support MFA for multiple users, the question is whether the agency should hold that client's login at all, or whether the client should add named agency users to its own account.

Checklist: before a client's security review

  • A list of every client platform the agency accesses, with the named person responsible for each
  • No shared logins where the platform allows individual users; where it does not, a password manager and a record of who has the credential
  • Multi-factor authentication on email, the file platform and every administrative account
  • A written offboarding step that removes a freelancer's or departing employee's access on the last day of the engagement
  • A rule that client files are kept in company-managed storage, not on personal drives or in personal cloud accounts
  • A tested restore of a recent campaign folder
  • A written policy on whether client material may be entered into public AI tools, agreed with each client's contract
  • A short incident plan stating who tells the client, and how fast

Larger clients may ask for a completed security questionnaire or an independent attestation report, such as a SOC 2 report. An agency that cannot produce one should say so plainly and offer what it has: the checklist above, a network and account description and a backup test result. Whether the agency should pursue a formal attestation is a business decision to make with the client and an independent auditor; an IT provider cannot supply one.

Large files, collaboration and software

Video and high-resolution assets make file transfer a daily constraint. The decision is where the working copy lives: shared storage that is backed up and has version history, so that an overwritten file can be recovered, or on local drives where it can be lost. Freelancers should receive access to specific folders with an end date rather than links that stay open indefinitely. Treat license management as an inventory task: a list of subscriptions by user, reviewed when people join or leave, avoids both unused spending and the surprise of an unlicensed seat on deadline day.

Email deliverability for agencies that send on behalf of clients

Agencies that run email campaigns should know Google's sender rules. Google's email sender guidelines require senders of more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail accounts to set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC authentication, to support one-click unsubscribe on marketing messages, and to keep the spam rate reported in Postmaster Tools below 0.30 percent. For each client domain, someone should check that these records exist and that the sending platform is authorized, because a campaign failing authentication harms the client's regular email as well.

Choosing IT support for a Los Angeles agency

Ask whether the provider has supported creative or professional services firms, and whether it will help with client security questionnaires by supplying accurate technical answers. Ask what happens when a deadline falls on a weekend, who handles a compromised social or ad account at 9 p.m., and how quickly the provider can rebuild a designer's laptop. Judge the arrangement by the number of administrative accounts without multi-factor authentication, time to remove a freelancer's access, the date of the last backup restore test, and the hours of creative time lost to IT problems each month.
